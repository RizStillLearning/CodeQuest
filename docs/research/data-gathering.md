## Analysis Results

### Respondent Background

- 14 respondents filled in the questionnaire. Half of them (7 people, 50%) are in semester 5. The rest are spread across semesters 1, 3, 7, and 8, and 2 have already graduated.
- Almost all have learned programming before (13 of 14 respondents, 92.9%). Experience levels vary: 6 people (42.9%) understand the basics but want to go deeper, 4 people (28.6%) are fairly skilled, and 4 people (28.6%) are still beginners or confused about the basics.
- The most wanted languages are Python and Java (8 people each, 57.1%), followed by C (5 people, 35.7%).
- The main learning sources are university/school classes and YouTube (11 people each, 78.6%), followed by self-study from documentation (6 people, 42.9%) and online courses (5 people, 35.7%).

### Respondent Challenges

The biggest problem is material that is too theoretical with too little practice (7 people, 50%). This is followed by getting bored quickly or studying inconsistently (6 people, 42.9%) and having no clear learning direction (4 people, 28.6%). This means respondents do not lack learning resources. They struggle to stay motivated and to find a structured learning order.

In addition, 3 respondents (21.4%) still find basic concepts such as variables and data types hard to understand. One respondent each also mentioned difficulty finding errors and the lack of feedback during practice questions. These numbers are small, but both issues relate directly to the practice question feature planned for CodeQuest.

### How CodeQuest Addresses This

CodeQuest aims to help respondents learn in a more structured and enjoyable way through levels, XP, and progress. Respondents received this concept well:

- **Interest in a game-style platform:** average 4.29 out of 5 (13 of 14 respondents gave a score of 4–5).
- **XP is the most motivating** (average 4.64; every respondent gave a score of 4–5), followed by the level-up-after-finishing-material system (4.43). The leaderboard scored lowest (4.21) and was the only one to receive a score of 2, so it is better suited as a supporting feature.
- **Most liked material:** interactive code examples (10 people, 71.4%), infographics/visuals (8 people, 57.1%), and short videos (6 people, 42.9%). This addresses the complaint about material being too theoretical.
- **Preferred practice questions:** predict the code output (9 people, 64.3%), short code fill-in and multiple choice (8 people each, 57.1%), and simple case studies (5 people, 35.7%).
- **Most important additional features:** progress tracking (11 people, 78.6%), discussion/Q&A forum (10 people, 71.4%), leaderboard (9 people, 64.3%), certificates/badges (6 people, 42.9%), and daily study reminders (3 people, 21.4%).
- **The user flow is easy to understand:** the flow Register → Choose Language → Level → Material → Questions → XP averaged 4.64, with all scores at 4–5. 10 respondents (71.4%) are willing to try the website and give feedback, the other 4 answered maybe, and no one declined.

### Challenges for CodeQuest

- **Technical stability.** Bugs, errors, and lag were named as reasons to quit by 4 of the 11 respondents who answered this question. Bugs that keep interrupting learning are seen as damaging the learning experience.
- **Keeping long-term interest.** 6 of 11 respondents said they might leave the platform if it feels boring or monotonous, if the features never change, or if it is hard to stay consistent with their progress. This matches the earlier "get bored quickly" complaint, so a game system alone is not enough. The content also needs variety.
- **Content must be relevant and kept up to date.** 3 respondents mentioned material that does not fit their needs, modules that are not updated, or running out of material. There was also a suggestion to add more programming languages.
- **User skill levels vary.** Respondents range from beginners (4 people) to fairly skilled (4 people), and 1 respondent named material that is too difficult for new users as a reason to quit. A tiered learning path and clear onboarding are needed.

## Conclusion

- **The problem is real.** Almost all respondents have learned programming, but they are still held back by material that is too theoretical (50%), getting bored quickly (42.9%), and having no clear learning direction (28.6%). CodeQuest's approach can address all three: interactive practice, gradual levels, and visible progress.
- **The gamification concept is strongly accepted.** XP (4.64), levels (4.43), interest in the platform (4.29), and ease of the flow (4.64) all scored high, and 10 of 14 respondents are willing to try it, with no one declining. Feature priorities: interactive code examples, predict-the-output/code fill-in/multiple-choice questions, progress tracking, and a discussion forum. The leaderboard, certificates/badges, and daily reminders fit as supporting features.
- **The biggest obstacle is the quality of the experience.** Bugs/errors/lag, monotonous content, and outdated modules are the main reasons respondents would quit. Stability, content variety, a tiered learning path, and regular material updates should be priorities.
- **Note.** There are only 14 respondents so far, and half of them are in semester 5. These results should be read as an early direction for development, not as a picture of all potential users.
